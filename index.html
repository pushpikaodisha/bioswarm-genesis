BioSwarm Genesis - Scientific Guide

1. Raycast Sensory Perception

Each organism projects directional raycast sensors from its head. Rays detect distances to food, conspecifics, predatory threats, scavengable carcasses, and coral barriers. These signals feed directly into the organism's neural network.

2. Synaptic Neural Network Brain

Organisms have a feedforward neural brain with 14 inputs, a hidden layer of interneurons, and 6 outputs controlling propulsion thrust, steering torque, sprint boost, mating intent, pheromone emission, and defensive quills.

3. Multi-Trophic Ecology

Herbivores graze on phytoplankton and golden nectar. Carnivores stalk and hunt other organisms. Scavengers recycle decomposing carcasses, detritus, and whale falls, possessing high armor and radiation resistance.

4. Day/Night & Seasonal Cycles

The biome rotates through 24-hour solar orbits and 4 dynamic seasons (Spring blooms, Summer heatwaves, Autumn detritus falls, and Winter glacial freezes) with real-time temperature fluctuations driving evolutionary adaptation.

5. Disease & Viral Dynamics

Virulent pathogens (Crimson Blight, Abyssal Flu, Neural Rot) spread via contact and water trails. Organisms with high immunity or survivors with acquired antibodies pass resistance down to their descendants.

6. Apex Leviathan Titan

A colossal abyssal behemoth that patrols the biome. Emits deep sonar shockwaves that scatter smaller swarms. When slain, it produces a monumental "Whale Fall" that nourishes the entire sea for generations!

7. Creature Lab & DNA Vault

Hand-craft custom organisms in the interactive Creature Lab with real-time micro-physics preview, then release into the wild. Preserve extraordinary mutations and landmark titans in the persistent DNA Vault and Fossil Record.
